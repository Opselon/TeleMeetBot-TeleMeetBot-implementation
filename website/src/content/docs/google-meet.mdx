TeleMeetBot drives the real Meet UI in headless Chromium. It is not an API client: it clicks the
same controls a person would, which is why the meeting must be one a guest can join.

## What the meeting must allow

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| Guest join (or a host who admits guests) | The bot has no Google account and does not sign in. |
| A host present, or a meeting that does not need admission | Without a host, Meet shows “no one can join unless invited or admitted”. |
| The Meet UI in English | Controls are matched by English labels; `browser.lang` defaults to `en-US`. |
| A meeting that is not sign-in-only | “Only users signed in to a Google account can join” cannot be satisfied. |

<Callout type="warn" title="Guest-only by design">
Meetings restricted to signed-in accounts, or to a specific organisation, cannot be joined. Waiting
in a lobby is supported; authenticating as a Google user is not.
</Callout>

## The join sequence

1. **Prejoin.** The bot fills `meet.guest_name`, mutes the camera and microphone (both by clicking the
   Meet toggles and by sending `Ctrl+E` / `Ctrl+D`), then clicks **Ask to join** or **Join now**.
2. **Lobby.** If the meeting requires admission, the session state becomes `admitting` and the bot
   re-requests entry every 30 seconds. A host clicking *Admit* is enough.
3. **In-call checks.** The engine confirms it is actually inside the call by looking for controls that
   only exist in a call — leave call, raise hand, people, chat, meeting details, share screen — not
   just the word “Present”.
4. **Present.** The bot clicks Meet's share control and confirms the capture started.

Muting before joining matters: Chromium's test camera device produces a green pattern, and Meet turns
the camera on by default on the prejoin screen. Muting both devices is what prevents a green tile for
everyone in the meeting.

## How presenting works

Meet has shipped two shapes of the same flow, and both are handled:

- a source menu (**A tab** / **A window** / **Entire screen**) that needs a second click, and
- a direct `getDisplayMedia` call from the toolbar button, after which Meet shows
  “Presentation is starting…”.

The presenter button is found among leaf buttons only, scored by exact `aria-label` / `data-tooltip`
phrases (`present now`, then `share screen`), and excludes audio, microphone, camera, leave and
settings controls. This matters because Meet's whole call-control bar is one `role="button"`
container whose text contains every label; clicking it opens nothing.

### Capture by tab title

Chromium answers an automatic tab capture by matching the **browser-side** tab title against
`--auto-select-tab-capture-source-by-title`. The bot therefore:

- serves the player from a loopback HTTP server titled `TeleMeet Media`,
- locks the title through the native `Document.prototype` setter so SPA title writes cannot change it,
- checks that Chromium itself sees the capture tab before clicking Present.

`meet.capture_source` must equal that title. Changing it without changing the player title breaks
presenting, and config validation rejects a screen-looking source while `present_mode` is `tab`.

<Callout type="tip">
The capture tab's title must be visible to Chromium, not only to JavaScript. If you fork the player
page, keep the title in the document head and avoid overriding `document.title` with a getter only.
</Callout>

## Sharing a display instead of a tab (screen mode)

Screen mode shares a virtual X display. It is useful when a page cannot be captured as a tab, but it
costs more resources and needs a display server:

```yaml
browser:
  headless: false
  display: ":99"
meet:
  present_mode: "screen"
  capture_source: "Entire screen (monitor 1)"
```

Start the bot under `xvfb-run`, or point `display` at an existing Xvfb display. Config validation
requires the capture source to look like a screen source in this mode. No-window Chromium cannot use
`getDisplayMedia` this way, so `headless` must be false.

## When the lobby does not let the bot in

The session stays in `admitting` and logs each attempt. It fails fast only for terminal conditions:
the meeting is full, the meeting has ended, or the code is invalid. Otherwise it keeps asking until
`meet.admit_wait_seconds` expires (default 300; `0` waits forever).

If the host never admits the bot, no amount of retrying helps. Ask the host to admit “TeleMeet Bot”,
or use a meeting that does not require admission.

## Recovery behaviour

- A YouTube consent wall that appears **after** the share is live is cleared in place, without
  re-presenting. The capture track is bound to the media tab, so restarting the share is unnecessary
  and risky.
- The bot re-presents only after two consecutive checks report that the presentation was lost, so a
  single slow DevTools call cannot cost a share.
- A transient DevTools hiccup no longer kills a live stream; the session requires repeated failures
  before it gives up.

## Limitations worth knowing

- **Meet changes its UI.** Selectors are label- and structure-based, with regression tests, but a
  redesign can still break a click. Run `--check --capture-test` after upgrading, and attach
  `logging.level: debug` output when reporting a failure.
- **Guest identity only.** The bot appears as “TeleMeet Bot” (or your `meet.guest_name`), not as a
  named Google account.
- **One presenter per session.** Two sessions in the same meeting both try to present; Meet permits
  one presenter, so the second will fail.
- **No camera compositing.** Tab mode shares the media tab only. Screen mode shares the whole display.
